Markets · duopoly as a learning dynamic

Duopoly price flows

Two firms set prices (or quantities). The first-order signal of a firm is its marginal profit: raise the price if that earns more, cut it if not. When both firms follow that signal the market flows to the Nash equilibrium, even though both would earn more inside the lens above it. Give each firm some weight on its rival's profit and the rest point slides toward the cartel.

Edgeworth cycles

Now make the goods identical and give each firm a capacity limit. Undercutting the rival wins the whole market, so best replies undercut, again and again, until the price is so low that serving the leftover demand at a high price pays more. The undercut firm relents, jumps back up, and the war restarts. There is no resting point.

Q-learning firms

Swap the gradient for a table. Each firm is a Q-learner that remembers last period's prices and learns by trial and error which price to charge in each of the 225 situations (Calvano, Calzolari, Denicolò and Pastorello, 2020). Such pairs usually settle well above the Nash price that first-order learners find, and they punish a price cut.

Why first-order learners don't collude

At the cartel prices each firm could earn more by shading its own price a little: its marginal profit there is negative. A firm that only follows the slope of its own profit therefore walks away from the lens toward the Nash equilibrium, where both slopes are zero. Collusion needs something beyond the first-order signal: weight on the rival's profit (the λ slider), memory and the threat of punishment (the Q-learners above), or learning that anticipates the other side's learning.

Models

Logit demand is the baseline of Calvano, Calzolari, Denicolò and Pastorello (2020): costs 1, qualities 2, outside good 0; μ sets how differentiated the products are, and the Nash and cartel prices are about 1.47 and 1.93 at μ = 0.25. Linear demand has qᵢ = 1 − pᵢ + γ pⱼ. Cournot firms choose quantities against the price 1 − q₁ − q₂. The Edgeworth model uses demand 1 − p, efficient rationing and alternating moves.
