Signaling games · everyone follows the first-order signal

Learning to signal

A sender sees the state of the world and picks a message. A receiver sees only the message and picks an action. Each of them nudges its own probabilities along the gradient of its own payoff. The sender has two probabilities and the receiver two more, so learning is a flow in four dimensions. These linked views take that flow apart.

What "first-order signal" means here

Every agent adjusts its own probabilities along the gradient of its own expected payoff, holding everyone else fixed. For the sender that gradient is π(t)·(value of message m in state t); for the receiver it is P(m)·E[payoff of action a | m]. Nobody models the other side; each just follows its local signal.

The learning rules differ in how they turn the signal into a step. Replicator is multiplicative weights in continuous time: the step is scaled by x(1−x). Projected gradient takes the raw gradient and stops at the walls. Softmax policy gradient is what a policy-gradient learner does in expectation; it slows down near pure strategies. Logit response jumps toward a smoothed best reply and is here for contrast.

Is this a genuine dynamical system?

Yes. In a 2×2 normal-form game each player is one number, so the state is a point in a square and the whole flow fits in one picture. Here the state is the pair (sender strategy, receiver strategy): four numbers for two states, twelve for three. Each agent's vector field depends on where the other agent is, which is why the tracers in the sender and receiver squares keep turning.

When interests are common (the Lewis game) the flow is secretly an ascent: all three gradient rules climb the shared payoff, which works as a potential. Equilibria are the tops of that landscape, and conventions form by rolling off a saddle. In the meaning plane with equal priors the common payoff is exactly (1 + dx·dy)/2. With conflicting interests (the handicap game) there is no potential, and play can circle forever.

Why language sometimes fails

A message nobody sends gives the receiver no signal at all, since its gradient is scaled by P(m). A receiver facing an uninformative sender learns to play the likelier state's action whatever it hears. Once it ignores messages, the sender's signal about which message to use is zero, and the pair freezes. With equal priors there is no likelier action, so this drift is absent and a convention always forms. With skewed priors, and especially with a receiver that learns faster than the sender, a sizeable share of runs freezes. A little exploration reopens the trap.

Why cheap faking cycles

In the handicap game both sender types want the reward and only the high type deserves it. If a display costs the low type less than the reward, mimicry pays while the receiver trusts displays. Mimicry then erodes the information in a display, trust falls, mimicry stops paying, and trust recovers. Replicator orbits close around a hybrid equilibrium; projected gradient settles on a limit cycle that touches the boundary. Raise the low type's cost above the reward (1) and honest signaling becomes stable.
