Normal-form games · two players, two actions

Two-by-two flows

With two actions a mixed strategy is one number, so a pair of learners is a point in the unit square and learning is a planar flow. Pick a game and a learning rule, then click the square to launch runs. The atlas on the right places every symmetric game on one map.

Reading the square

The horizontal axis is the probability that the row player uses its first action, the vertical axis the same for the column player. Corners are pure profiles. The grey tracers follow the learning flow; where they bunch up and stall there is a rest point. Filled markers are stable, half-filled are saddles, a ringed dot is a neutral center that orbits circle around.

The dashed lines are where a player is indifferent between its two actions. The row player's probability can only turn around on its line, the column player's on its own. Tints show which stable outcome each starting point reaches under the current rule.

Clocks matter

In Matching Pennies the continuous replicator dynamics conserves a quantity, so every orbit closes. Switch on Discrete steps and the same signal, applied with a fixed step, overshoots on every turn and spirals out to the boundary. Tick Optimistic and each player extrapolates its signal one step ahead; the spiral turns inward. Nothing about the game changed, only how the first-order signal is used.

One population or two

The diagonal of the square is what a single population playing against itself would do. In Chicken the mixed equilibrium is stable along the diagonal but a saddle in the square: when row and column players learn separately, any asymmetry grows and one side ends up yielding.
